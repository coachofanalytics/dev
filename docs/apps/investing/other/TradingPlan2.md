Investor Plan Structures

CODA offers multiple managed investment plans, each tailored to different investor goals, return targets, and risk tolerances. All plans employ CODA’s algorithmic options strategies but with varying emphasis on income vs. growth and different fee structures. Below we outline each plan’s key parameters – minimum capital, targeted returns, fee model (management and performance fees), risk profile, and recommended capital lock-in period:

CODA Income Plan

The Income Plan focuses on generating stable monthly cash flow for investors who prioritize income over aggressive growth. It employs conservative options strategies (such as selling covered calls, cash-secured puts, and credit spreads) designed to earn premium income regularly. This plan targets a relatively lower volatility and steady return profile, making it suitable for income-oriented investors (e.g. retirees or those seeking regular withdrawals).

Minimum Capital: $10,000 (recommended minimum to efficiently deploy income strategies; the system requires at least $5,000 to open any managed account, and $10K+ ensures meaningful monthly payouts).

Target Returns: ~1%–1.5% per month net to the client (around 12–18% annual). For example, the plan aims for roughly $420 net income monthly on a $30K portfolio, scaling proportionally with account size. This level is pursued while keeping risk low and consistent.

Fee Model: Low management fee, typically around 1% annually (charged monthly/quarterly) and a modest performance fee (e.g. 10% of profits above a hurdle rate). The performance fee uses a high-water mark with a preferred return hurdle – for instance, no performance fee on the first ~8% annual return so that clients get a base income first. Only profits beyond that threshold are subject to the performance split. (The system supports configurable fee tiers per account, and the Income Plan generally has one of the more client-friendly fee schedules.)

Risk Profile: Conservative. The Income Plan prioritizes capital preservation. It uses tight risk limits per account – for example, maximum daily loss ~1% and monthly loss ~5% of account value. Position sizes are small and well-diversified; no single trade will typically use more than ~10% of the portfolio capital. Strategies employed (credit spreads, covered positions) have defined-risk and the system strictly enforces stop-loss rules to avoid large drawdowns.

Capital Lock-in: 3 months minimum suggested. While funds can be withdrawn with notice, a quarter is recommended to allow the income strategy to cycle through multiple option expirations and realize targeted returns. Frequent withdrawals are discouraged, as the strategy works best when capital is continuously deployed. (There are no long lock-up commitments for the retail Income Plan, but short-term withdrawals may forfeit current cycle’s accrued income.)

CODA Growth Plan

The Growth Plan is geared towards investors seeking capital appreciation and higher returns, with a willingness to accept more volatility. This plan employs more aggressive options strategies and may take directional positions or leverage when appropriate. It still uses algorithmic trade selection and risk controls, but with a higher risk tolerance than the Income Plan.

Minimum Capital: $20,000 (to ensure sufficient buffer for margin and to diversify across multiple positions – though the platform minimum is $5K, Growth Plan’s strategies benefit from at least $20K to absorb short-term volatility).

Target Returns: ~2%–3% per month on average (approximately 24–36% annual) net to the client, outpacing the Income Plan. The Growth Plan reinvests profits to compound capital. The higher return target is achieved by strategies such as wider credit spreads, selective long calls/puts (on a limited basis), and leveraging volatility when opportunities arise.

Fee Model: Standard hedge-fund style fees – around 2% annual management fee (billed monthly or quarterly) and 20% performance fee on new net profits (again calculated with a high-water mark to ensure fees are only on profits beyond the previous high). No performance fee is taken if the account is below its high-water mark (i.e., recovering past losses). The hurdle for the Growth Plan may be set at a modest level (e.g. a few percent) or just the high-water mark with no fixed hurdle, since growth investors primarily care about net absolute returns. Management fees cover the active management and technology resources required for the more frequent trading in this plan.

Risk Profile: Moderate to High. The Growth Plan tolerates higher short-term drawdowns in pursuit of larger gains. Risk limits might be set at, for example, ~2–3% max daily loss and ~10% max monthly loss, higher than the Income Plan. Trades may include higher delta positions and occasional naked options (limited) or leveraged ETFs, under strict monitoring. The algorithm still enforces risk rules (stop-loss at 200% option premium, etc.), but the portfolio will ride through normal market swings. The expected volatility of returns is higher, and investors should have a multi-month outlook.

Capital Lock-in: 6 months recommended. Given the higher volatility strategy, we advise clients to commit their capital for at least half a year to allow the strategy to play out and to smooth out monthly variations. Withdrawals can be accommodated with notice, but withdrawing too early could mean missing the rebound after a drawdown. Performance fees, if any, are typically crystallized semi-annually or annually for Growth accounts to ensure alignment over the longer term.

Overall, the fee model is designed to align CODA’s interests with the client’s: management fees provide baseline revenue for CODA (covering operational costs) while performance fees ensure CODA is rewarded when clients profit beyond a healthy threshold. The high-water mark means CODA won’t charge performance fees on the same gains twice or during recovery from a loss. This structure aims to be fair and motivating: clients get a solid share of profits (typically 75–80% of gross profits, after fees) and CODA earns upside only when it delivers superior results.

Case Study: SPY Credit Spread Trade

To illustrate CODA’s trading strategy and risk management in action, consider a real-world example: an options trade on the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (SPY) using a credit spread. We will walk through the trade setup, the exit logic, the profit/loss math, breakeven analysis, and how the strategy is refined over time.

Trade Setup: CODA’s algorithm identifies an opportunity to generate income on SPY through a bull put credit spread (a bullish options strategy). At the time of entry, suppose SPY is trading around $450. The system sells an out-of-the-money put option at a $440 strike (expiring in 30 days) and buys a protective put at a $435 strike (same expiration) to limit risk. This short put vertical spread is opened for a net credit of about $1.00 per share, or $100 premium collected per contract. The choice of strikes and expiration aligns with CODA’s entry criteria – for instance, the short put’s delta is roughly 0.30 (30% probability in the money) which is within the target range of 0.25–0.35, the days to expiration ~30 days which is in the optimal 30–45 day window, and implied volatility is adequately high (IV Rank > 30%) to make option-selling attractive. There are no imminent earnings or major news for SPY within this period, and technical signals show bullish/neutral momentum (“price action favorable” per the algorithm’s checklist). By structuring a $5-wide put spread, CODA defines the maximum risk while generating immediate income from the premium. The maximum profit on this trade is $100 (the credit received) and the maximum loss is $400 (the $5 spread width minus $1 credit, times 100 shares) per contract. These parameters are known at entry: the trade is defined-risk. According to options fundamentals, the max profit equals the premium collected, and the max loss is limited to the difference between the strike prices minus that premium

. The breakeven price at expiration is $(440 - 1.00) = $439.00 – if SPY is above $439 at expiry, the position is profitable for the client

Exit Logic: CODA does not simply “set and forget” this trade until expiration. The platform continuously monitors it via the OptionsMonitoringService and RiskManagementService. The exit rules for credit spreads are pre-defined: the system will take profits early if possible and cut losses to avoid the worst-case. In this case, CODA’s rules specify a 50% profit target – meaning if the spread’s value declines to about $0.50 (so that we’ve earned half of the maximum $1.00 profit, i.e. $50 per contract gained), the system will alert to close the trade and lock in profits. This often happens when SPY rises or time decay erodes the option premium sufficiently. On the flip side, a stop-loss is set at 200% of the premium, which here translates to the spread price reaching $3.00 (since initial $1.00 credit, if the spread costs $3.00 to buy back, the trade would incur a $2.00 loss, which is 200% of the $1.00 credit). In practice, that means if SPY falls sharply and the short $440 put goes deep in-the-money, the system will trigger a closing order when the loss threshold is hit, rather than risk the full $400 max loss. Additional exit criteria are also in place: for example, if 5 days remain to expiration and the trade is still open, CODA may close it to avoid gamma risk and unpredictable swings in the final days. Or if SPY’s delta suddenly jumps beyond 0.50 (indicating the trade has become too directional), the system will consider an exit or adjustment. These rules are enforced through automated alerts and require action (the system can auto-close critical conditions, denoted as “REQUIRE: Close immediately” in the logic). In summary, CODA’s exit strategy for this SPY spread is well-defined: take profits around $50 gain, cut losses around $200 loss, or exit earlier if nearing expiration or if risk metrics (like delta) exceed comfortable levels.

Return Math & Outcome: Let’s suppose SPY indeed remains stable to slightly bullish over the next few weeks. After 15 days, assume SPY has drifted up to $455 and implied volatility has dropped – the put spread we sold for $1.00 is now trading at about $0.30. This means we could buy it back for $30, realizing a profit of $70 per contract. This is 70% of the maximum profit. According to our rules, once profit hit 50% of max (which would have been at $0.50 price), an alert was triggered to take profit. The portfolio manager (or an automated rule) decides to close the trade at $0.30 to secure the gain. The final result: $70 profit on $430 of capital at risk (since initially $500 was locked as margin minus $70 now realized means $430 risk was actually utilized), which is about 16.3% return on risk in 15 days. Annualized, that’s a very high return, but this trade is just one short-term example. Alternatively, had SPY remained above $440 through expiration, and we held the spread, we would have kept the full $100 (a 25% return on the $400 risk). CODA often prefers to realize profits early rather than aiming for the last dollar, to free up capital and reduce risk. Now consider the downside case: if SPY had instead dropped to $430 shortly after entry, the spread’s value might spike to, say, $3.00 or more. The system’s stop-loss trigger at $3.00 would fire, and CODA would close the spread, limiting the loss to roughly $200 per contract. That is half the max loss, meaning we saved the client from the full $400 loss scenario. This controlled loss is an example of CODA’s risk management in action – by adhering to the 200% stop rule, the portfolio avoided a larger drawdown. The breakeven point of $439 helped guide our risk: if SPY had hovered near $439 at expiration, the trade would roughly break even (small profit or loss depending on time value). In practice, CODA would not hold that long in uncertainty – it would have exited earlier either at a modest profit or a small loss.

Strategy Refinement: Each trade like this SPY spread is a learning opportunity that feeds back into CODA’s algorithms. CODA’s platform tracks detailed performance analytics for every position (win rate, average profit, loss, drawdown, etc.). In this case, suppose the trade was profitable. The system logs the conditions: SPY’s trend, volatility level, option Greeks, Unusual Whales flow sentiment at entry, etc. If similar conditions arise, the PositionRankingService may score a similar trade highly in the future. Conversely, if the trade hit the stop-loss, the system analyzes what went wrong – e.g., was there a news event or did the volatility spike unexpectedly? CODA’s team might refine the strategy by adjusting rules: for instance, tightening the stop-loss or avoiding spreads during certain volatile periods. They could also incorporate Unusual Whales data more: if large bearish options flow on SPY was detected (which CODA’s integration can catch), maybe the trade could have been avoided. The strategy refinement process is continuous. CODA has a dedicated risk management module that reviews outcomes and can suggest rule tweaks (like lowering the delta threshold or adding a time-based scale-out). The platform’s AI might, for example, learn that exiting at 40% profit is statistically more optimal for SPY spreads and update the profit-taking rule. Additionally, post-trade review might reveal that a different strike selection (e.g., selling the 435/430 put spread instead) would have been more efficient; such insights get incorporated into the trade recommendation engine. Finally, on a portfolio level, CODA examines how this SPY trade correlates with other positions – ensuring that the next time, if multiple trades are on, the risk is spread across sectors or ticker, etc. Over time, these refinements aim to increase the win rate and average return per trade while reducing risk, exemplifying CODA’s commitment to data-driven improvement of its options strategies.

BRD – Business Requirements Document

The CODA platform has been specified with clear business requirements to support a managed options trading service for multiple clients. Key modules and capabilities include:

Multi-Client Account Management: The system must support multiple client accounts, each isolated and managed separately. This allows CODA to trade options for many clients with different plans concurrently. Each account has its own settings (risk limits, fee tier, assigned manager, etc.), and clients can only view their own accounts. CODA staff (admins/portfolio managers) can administer all accounts as needed. This requirement ensures scalability of the business (BR-001) – CODA can onboard new clients easily without interference between accounts.

Trade Management Module: A core requirement is the ability to create and manage options positions for each client account (i.e., the trade lifecycle). Portfolio managers need interfaces to enter new trades (choosing strategy templates like spreads, covered calls, etc.), record executions, and track open positions. The system supports all common options strategies – e.g. cash-secured puts, covered calls, vertical spreads (bull put, bear call), iron condors – as well as limited directional trades. Trade management includes validations (e.g. ensure an order doesn’t violate risk limits or an account’s buying power). There are also features for bulk operations (closing or rolling multiple positions) to efficiently manage portfolios. In essence, this module implements trading rules and position bookkeeping as defined in functional requirements (FR-002, FR-006, FR-007).

Portfolio Tracking & Risk Monitoring: The platform continuously tracks each account’s portfolio performance and risk metrics in real time. This includes updating positions’ market values, calculating P&L (unrealized and realized), and aggregating risk exposures across the account. Risk monitoring is crucial: the system monitors if any account is breaching defined risk limits (daily/weekly/monthly loss thresholds, concentration limits, etc.) and generates alerts accordingly. For example, if an account’s total unrealized loss exceeds, say, 5% in a week or if any single position hits its stop-loss condition, an alert is sent to managers (and possibly to the client, depending on settings). The business requires that clients’ capital is safeguarded by these checks – no single trade or day should jeopardize the entire account beyond agreed limits. This maps to BR-004 (performance tracking) and the risk management aspects of FR-003. Essentially, portfolio tracking provides transparency (so clients and managers know the account status at any time) and risk management provides safety nets (automatic alerts and halts when necessary).

Fee Management System: CODA’s business model relies on an automated fee computation and tracking system. The platform must calculate management fees (e.g., on a monthly or quarterly schedule) and performance fees (when applicable) for each account accurately. This includes applying the correct fee percentages per the client’s plan or tier, using high-water mark logic for performance fees, generating invoices or statements for fees, and possibly integrating with payment systems. The system keeps a ledger of fees accrued and paid, ensuring transparency for both CODA and clients. For instance, at month-end, the system might deduct the pro-rata management fee from the account or mark it payable, and at quarter-end calculate performance fee based on the account’s new high-water mark. All of this is done in an auditable way (the requirement was to maintain historical fee records and automate invoice generation). The FeeTierConfiguration model allows different fee structures (CODA has 5 configurable tiers) to be assigned to each account, supporting the Income/Growth/Premium variations and volume discounts for larger accounts. This module addresses BR-002.

Client Reporting and Dashboard: Providing clients with comprehensive, transparent reporting is a critical business need (BR-003). Each client has access to a dashboard (read-only) where they can see their account’s performance in real time. Key information shown includes current account balance, open positions with details (symbols, strategies, P&L, days to expiration, etc.), and performance charts (e.g. account value over time, monthly returns, win/loss stats). In addition to the live dashboard, CODA delivers regular reports: typically weekly performance summaries and a more detailed monthly statement. These reports break down the account’s returns, fees, and notable trades, giving clients full transparency into what CODA is doing on their behalf. The system must generate these reports automatically and make them available for download (PDF or via email). For example, a monthly statement might include an income vs. target comparison (for Income Plan clients, showing how actual income stacks against the $420/mo target), a summary of all trades closed during the month, fees charged, and the ending balance. The business requirement is that clients feel informed and trust the process, so the reporting module is designed to be comprehensive and user-friendly.

Investor Plan Configuration: The platform needs to support multiple plan structures (as detailed in the section above) within one system. This means business logic to handle different minimums, lock-ins, and preferences. For instance, if a client is in the Income Plan, the system knows to target income trades and perhaps show an “income dashboard” highlighting monthly income vs target. If another client is in Growth Plan, the system might show growth metrics instead. While much of this is internal configuration, it is a business requirement that CODA can market and operate distinct plans seamlessly on one platform. This includes having correct disclosures and settings for each (e.g., Premium Plan clients get the option for premium alerts, which standard clients do not). Roles and entitlements in the system also reflect plan differences (for example, an internal flag might allow a Premium client to see trade alerts). This configurability ensures CODA can service various products without needing separate systems.

Data Integrations for Market Edge: CODA integrates with external platforms and data sources to enhance its trading decisions – a business imperative to maintain an edge. Unusual Whales is a service for unusual options flow; CODA uses this to gauge market sentiment and identify big money trades that could influence its own strategy. The system’s requirements included connecting to these services via API or data feeds. OptionsPlay data might be used to quickly evaluate strategy P&L or probability. By integrating these platforms, CODA adds a layer of intelligence beyond standard market data. The business needs these integrations to improve trade selection and justify the “AI-enhanced” aspect of the service. User Roles and Access: The platform defines clear user roles to support the business workflow and security. Primarily, the roles are: Client (Investor), Portfolio Manager (Trader), and Administrator. Final Recommendations and Scaling Strategy

As CODA moves forward, here are final recommendations to scale the business from the current $30K–$100K account sizes to a larger client base and higher AUM, while maintaining performance and managing costs:

Gradual Scaling of Accounts: The system is already designed to handle up to 100 accounts and hundreds of positions. To scale from $30K accounts to $100K+ accounts, CODA should adjust position sizing algorithms and risk limits dynamically. Larger accounts can support more positions, so use the capital allocation engine to increase the number of concurrent trades rather than simply making trades larger. For example, maintain the rule of ~10% allocation per trade – a $100K account would run more independent positions than a $30K account, improving diversification and smoothing returns. The platform should be tested for performance as positions grow (ensuring that risk calculations and data updates remain fast with more positions – adding caching or optimizations as needed). It’s recommended to stage the onboarding of larger accounts in phases and monitor system load, adjusting the infrastructure (DB indexes, CPU/memory resources for workers, etc.) according to scaling needs.

Enhanced Automation (Broker Integration): To onboard many more clients without linear increases in operational workload, CODA should prioritize integrating a broker API (as planned in Phase 4). Executing trades across dozens of accounts can be time-consuming if done manually; an API integration will allow CODA to send trade orders to all client accounts programmatically, ensuring timely and identical execution. This is critical as account sizes increase – institutional clients will expect efficient execution. It will also reduce errors and free up portfolio managers to focus on strategy rather than manual order entry. Along with this, implementing Celery workers for any heavy tasks (trade simulations, batch reporting) will help maintain responsiveness as the load grows.

Refine Fee Structures for High AUM: As CODA scales to larger accounts and more clients, re-evaluate the fee model to stay competitive and ensure client satisfaction. The current tiered fee structure (0–3% mgmt, 0–30% perf configurable in 5 tiers) is flexible. For example, for accounts $100K and above, CODA might consider offering slightly lower performance fee percentages or reduced management fee (e.g. 1.5% instead of 2%), to attract high net-worth and institutional money. Because larger accounts still yield more absolute fee revenue, volume discounts can be justified. The system already supports such tier adjustments via FeeTierConfiguration, so implementing new tiers or adjusting thresholds is straightforward. Ensuring the fee hurdle (preferred return) stays in place (like the 8% example) is also important for marketing to bigger clients – they will appreciate that CODA only takes performance fees when truly adding value above a standard return.

Client Acquisition and Upsell: To efficiently grow AUM, focus on upselling existing clients and cost-effective onboarding of new ones:

Utilize the Scenario Reports & Capital Upsell feature (ER-023) to show clients how much more income or return they could achieve by increasing their capital with CODA. For instance, a client with $30K might receive a personalized WhatsApp report illustrating that adding $20K could increase their monthly income from $420 to ~$700, projecting scenarios in an easy-to-grasp way. This encourages organic growth of accounts and is relatively low-cost (since it’s just communication based on existing performance data).

Develop a streamlined onboarding process (perhaps through a web sign-up or an application form integrated into the platform) to handle new clients without requiring heavy manual effort. This could include an online risk questionnaire that automatically assigns the client to Income vs Growth plan (mirroring the InvestorRiskProfile model usage), e-signature for agreements, and automatic account creation in the system. Reducing the friction in onboarding will allow CODA to add clients more quickly.

Emphasize referral programs – satisfied clients with growing accounts can refer others. The system can track referrals and maybe offer a fee discount for referrals (this can be handled via FeeTier adjustments on the referred accounts).

Maintain Performance via AI & Analytics: As the number of accounts and total capital increases, it’s vital to maintain trading performance (so that returns scale with AUM and don’t regress). Continue to invest in the AI and analytics side of the platform:

The PositionScoringService should be continuously improved (Phase 3/4 plans to incorporate ML predictions). With more data gathered from live trading, CODA can train its models to better predict which trades will be winners. This will help deploy the larger capital more effectively, choosing the best opportunities among many.

Implement Advanced Analytics and BI (business intelligence) for internal use. For example, track strategy performance across all accounts to identify which strategies are most scalable. If covered calls are yielding consistent results but iron condors are faltering at scale, the team can adjust allocations. The plan for Advanced Analytics (ER-007) and performance metrics (FR-008) will support this by providing insights like profit factor by strategy and best/worst performing symbols.

Keep enhancing risk management as well – consider dynamic risk limits that adjust based on volatility regimes (ER-014). For instance, if the market enters a high-volatility period, the system could automatically tighten risk limits (maybe reduce max allocation per position or raise the stop-loss strictness) across all accounts to protect capital. Conversely, in calm markets maybe slightly more risk can be taken. This adaptability will be important when managing larger pools of money in varying market conditions.

Cost Management and Recovery: With scaling, CODA should monitor its own cost structure. More clients mean more data usage (API calls), potentially higher brokerage fees for more trades, and increased operational overhead. Here are strategies:

Optimize External Data Costs: Unusual Whales API, for example, might charge per call – implement caching and batched requests as planned to avoid redundant costs. If the AUM grows significantly, consider negotiating enterprise deals with data providers or using more open-source data to reduce subscription costs.

Leverage Economies of Scale in Trades: When accounts have similar positions (as often they will), trade execution can be aggregated. If five clients need the same SPY spread, execute it in bulk or block trading and allocate fills, rather than five separate orders. This can reduce slippage and commission costs. Introducing a trade allocation system (perhaps as an enhancement) where CODA’s traders enter a single master trade and the system allocates it across accounts pro-rata can be very efficient.

Ensure performance fees cover costs: One definition of “cost-recovery” is making sure that the performance fees at least cover the operational costs for each client.
