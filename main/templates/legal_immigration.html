Frequently Asked Questions

Green card processing times vary significantly depending on the category and country of origin. Family-based applications typically take 6-24 months, while employment-based applications may take 1-7 years. Check USCIS processing times online for your specific category and location.

Generally, you need an Employment Authorization Document (EAD) to work legally. You may be eligible to apply for an EAD while your application is pending if you can demonstrate financial hardship or meet specific category requirements. Consult an immigration attorney for your specific situation.

A Notice to Appear means you're in removal proceedings. You MUST respond by the deadline listed. Immediately consult with an immigration attorney. Do not ignore the notice as this can result in automatic deportation. Many legal aid organizations offer free consultations.

You can apply for asylum by filing Form I-589 within one year of arriving in the U.S. If in removal proceedings, notify your immigration judge. You must demonstrate persecution based on race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in a social group. Consult an attorney to strengthen your application.

You have the right to remain silent, refuse searches of your home without a warrant, and speak to an attorney. Say: "I do not consent to a search" and "I want to speak to a lawyer." Do not sign anything without understanding it or without legal advice. These rights apply regardless of immigration status.

You must file a Petition for Alien Relative (Form I-130) and be able to meet income requirements. You'll need to file an Affidavit of Support (Form I-864). Processing times vary by relationship (spouse, parent, sibling). Consult USCIS.gov for current procedures and timelines.

Generally, you should not travel outside the U.S. while your green card is pending as it may be viewed as abandonment of your residence. If you must travel, you may be able to file for Advance Parole. Consult an attorney before making this decision.

Costs vary by category and can include USCIS filing fees ($640-$1,025), medical exam fees ($150-$500), police clearance fees, and potentially attorney fees ($1,500-$3,000+). Some individuals may qualify for fee waivers. Check the most current fees on USCIS.gov.
